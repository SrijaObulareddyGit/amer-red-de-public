1. Executive Summary

Scope. 70 open Digital Engagement (DE) cases spread across 51 AMER red accounts, derived from Cloud__c = 'Service-Digital Engagement' on Cases where Open_Red_Account__c = true and Account.Region__c = 'AMER'. Adjacent clouds (Industry-OmniStudio, Mulesoft-Omni Gateway, Marketing App-Messaging) are explicitly excluded — they are different products from Service Cloud DE.

Headline findings:

  1. DE is a confirmed red-driver on 11 AMER accounts, probable on 30, potential on 0, and unverified on 10. See classification rules at the top of each section.
  2. Omni-Channel routing is the single biggest DE theme. 32 of 70 open DE cases reference routing / queue / AgentWork / PendingServiceRouting / UserServicePresence symptoms. 3 of the 6 Level 1 Critical open DE cases (two accounts with stuck chat sessions; one with an unresponsive agent box tied to session lifecycle) sit in this theme.
  3. Release regressions are concentrated on Messaging Channel + Flow. One active L1 is a direct major-release regression (Channel Type field being recognized as Object instead of Picklist by Flow Compiler). The customer explicitly notes a prior September 2026 case that was expected to ship a fix before production and did not. That one case is the clearest preventable red trigger in the whole AMER DE portfolio.
  4. Multiple accounts carry 5+ year DE case histories. Nine accounts each carry 139-271 lifetime DE cases dating back 4-5+ years. On accounts of this size the question is less "did a case cause red" and more "did DE instability prevent them from getting out of red." For those accounts, the DE share of open red metric is the honest answer: if DE is <30% of the account's open red volume, DE is contributing but not dominant.
  5. "Closed but recurring" is a repeated failure mode. One Sev 1 (CASE-0008) was opened solely to ask the ETA after the parent case CASE-REDACTED closed without durable fix. Another L1 subject ends with "3" — meaning it is the third sibling case on the same BYOC Einstein-summarization symptom. These two represent a recurring hygiene gap: closing cases without a stability window or product-side tracking.

Red-reason alignment. DE-caused red classification — what each tier means

CONFIRMEDDE is a cause of this account's red status. On-record evidence directly ties the account's red state to Digital Engagement — a severity-1 outage, multiple severity-2 incidents, or DE dominating the open-red volume. These accounts should be reported out as DE-driven in leadership reviews.
Rule: ≥1 L1 Critical open DE case; OR ≥2 L2 Urgent open DE cases; OR DE ≥ 50% of the account's open red volume with ≥3 open DE cases.
PROBABLEDE is reasonably suspected of contributing — not yet proven as the primary driver. There is meaningful DE signal on the account (an urgent DE case, a sustained volume of DE cases, or DE representing a sizable share of open red) but the evidence stops short of CONFIRMED. These accounts warrant a product-level health check before escalating to "DE-caused."
Rule: ≥1 L2 Urgent open DE case; OR ≥5 open DE cases; OR DE ≥ 30% of open red volume.
POTENTIALDE is a contributing factor but not demonstrated as primary. Low-to-moderate DE case activity with no high-severity drivers.
Rule: 2–4 open DE cases, mixed severity, < 30% DE share.
UNVERIFIEDInsufficient DE evidence to attribute red status. The account has one open DE case — not enough on its own to call the account DE-driven.
Rule: 1 open DE case — insufficient to attribute red status.
How to read this for leadership: treat the Confirmed + Probable accounts as the DE focus list. Confirmed are the ones to stand behind as DE-caused today; Probable are the ones to validate with product telemetry and customer conversations before claiming DE causation. Potential and Unverified have DE cases on the books but should not be reported out as DE-driven without further investigation.
